# Combat-sport strength and conditioning: research notes

Checked: 2026-11-14. Purpose: source material for a future training-plan discussion.
This is a targeted search, not a systematic review, an approved program, or a change to the
authoring kit's doctrine. No account assignment or cartridge change is implied.
Personal intake answers and account identifiers are deliberately excluded.

## Professional practice

### UFC Performance Institute: current public curriculum

[Official specialist S&C curriculum](https://ufc-performance-institute-s-school.teachable.com/p/ufcpi-certification-specialist-sc2)
and [official service description](https://www.ufcpi.com/).

The curriculum separates strength, power, speed, aerobic, glycolytic and alactic development,
and includes testing, needs analysis and integration with MMA. The service description says
individual performance plans complement MMA training. These public descriptions establish
the framework being taught; they do not disclose the paid course content or an individual
fighter's program. No publication date was shown for the curriculum.

### Beattie and Ruddock, June 2025: boxing coaching-practice survey

[Strength Training Practices in Amateur and Professional Boxing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40403184/)
— DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000005088.

Seventy-two trainers/support staff reported their practices. Punch-specific and reactive
strength work was common; maximal strength was programmed least despite recognition of its
potential value. Concern about unwanted muscle mass was one reported barrier. This describes
practice and beliefs, not causal evidence that the most popular methods work best. Abstract
reviewed; full manuscript access was blocked.

## Broad training evidence published in 2026

### ACSM position stand, March 2026

[Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
— DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897.

Synthesizes 137 reviews, with searches through October 2024. Heavier loading and at least two
weekly sessions favored strength; moderate loads with fast lifting intent supported power;
higher weekly volume favored hypertrophy. Failure was unnecessary. These are healthy-adult
findings, not a tested prescription for professional fighters or a guarantee of zero muscle
growth. Exact effort targets and individual exercise choices still require judgment.

### Held et al., February 2026: concurrent training

[Maximizing Adaptations in Concurrent Training](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02401-y).

This umbrella review supports combining endurance and resistance training. Its abstract
reports no clear overall sequence effect and highlights scarce elite-athlete data. It does
not establish a universal required gap between sessions. Abstract reviewed; full text was
subscription-only. Publication in 2026 does not mean its underlying trials were conducted in 2026.

### Advanced resistance-training methods, April 2026

[Systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-026-02428-1)
— DOI: 10.1007/s40279-026-02428-1.

No clear general strength/power advantage emerged for advanced methods over traditional
training. Most participants had limited lifting experience; elite extrapolation is uncertain.
Complex pairings and specialist equipment therefore need a practical reason, rather than
being compulsory features of a fighter's program.

## Direct boxing intervention evidence

### Tian et al., June 2026: repeated heavy-bag bursts

[Four-week trial in elite male boxers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1839505/full)
— DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2026.1839505.

Sixteen athletes were randomized. Added brief maximal bag bursts improved upper-body
anaerobic test performance. Some punching outcomes did not show statistically significant
between-group differences. Small sample, short duration and measurement limitations make
the findings preliminary; this is not evidence to copy the entire protocol into a busy week.
The [July correction](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2026.1925581) concerns an equipment photograph
and AI disclosure, not an intervention change.

### Han et al., August 2026: velocity-based lifting

[Eight-week collegiate-boxer trial, companion report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13510493/)
— DOI: 10.3390/jfmk11030310.

Twenty-eight male boxers lifted twice weekly. A low velocity-loss approach showed promising
short-duration punching outcomes versus fixed-repetition training. Outcomes were exploratory,
registration was retrospective, measurement reliability was uncertain and training-dose records
were incomplete. Stopping explosive work before substantial slowing is a reasonable coaching
inference, not proof that a particular threshold or sensor is required.

### Cinarli et al., September 2026: additional boxing HIIT

[Cardiorespiratory adaptations trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42760651/)
— DOI: 10.1097/MD.0000000000050694.

Sixteen trained young participants completed four weeks. Extra HIIT improved selected pulmonary
measures, but aerobic-fitness and heart-rate-recovery changes were not clearly superior between
groups. The HIIT group also trained more overall. More intervals are not automatically better.
Abstract reviewed.

## Working interpretation for later design

These are provisional coaching judgments, not directly tested conclusions about any one person:

- Develop strength, explosive power, aerobic capacity and repeat-effort endurance deliberately.
- Count classes and bag work in total training load before adding conditioning finishers.
- Make short lifting sessions efficient by limiting exercise count and redundant sets;
  preserve enough rest to maintain the intended performance.
- A third weekly lifting session can be smaller if it shares time with demanding bag work.
- Direct chest and calf work can be included according to preference and recovery capacity.
- Distinguish technical bag rounds from power practice and demanding conditioning intervals.
- Choose progression and recovery weeks from actual training response, not a copied pro schedule.

Before authoring: confirm current training tolerance, class content/intensity, current equipment,
relevant loading limitations, benchmarks and what a successful conditioning improvement means.

## Accessory-growth follow-up, checked 2026-11-14

- [Chaves et al., 2020: horizontal versus incline pressing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7449336/).
  An eight-week study in untrained men found greater growth at one upper-pectoral measurement
  site with incline pressing, with no difference at two other sites. Incline pressing is a
  reasonable upper-chest choice, but exact regional growth in experienced athletes is uncertain.
- [Kinoshita et al., 2023: standing versus seated calf raises](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10753835/).
  A small 12-week within-person study in untrained adults favored standing raises for
  gastrocnemius growth; soleus growth was similar between conditions. Seated performance alone
  does not establish a complete calf-growth prescription. Machine loads are apparatus-specific.

Application: targeted hypertrophy work can be a secondary goal within a performance-oriented
program. A modest starting accessory dose, progressed against recovery and performance, is a
coaching choice rather than a trial-proven optimum for fighters. Do not present a weekly set
count as a universal minimum or promise precise changes to muscle shape.
